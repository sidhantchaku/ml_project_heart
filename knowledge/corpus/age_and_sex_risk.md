---
title: Age and Sex as Cardiovascular Risk Factors
source_organization: CDC
source_url: https://www.cdc.gov/heart-disease/risk-factors/index.html
category: risk_factors
---

Risk of heart disease rises with age for both men and women. Most people who
die of coronary heart disease are 65 or older. Before age 55, men generally
face a higher risk than women of the same age; after menopause, women's risk
increases and gradually approaches that of men, in part because natural
estrogen levels drop and estrogen appears to offer some protective effect on
blood vessels.

Family history also matters alongside age: a person whose parent or sibling
had early heart disease (before age 55 for a male relative, or 65 for a
female relative) has a higher personal risk, independent of other factors.
Age and family history cannot be changed, which is why modifiable factors
such as blood pressure, cholesterol, activity level, and smoking status
matter more the older a person gets.

Being in an older age range does not by itself mean a diagnosis -- it is one
input among several that a clinician weighs together with symptoms, test
results, and personal and family history.
