High blood pressure (hypertension) makes the heart work harder than it
should and gradually damages the walls of the arteries, which contributes to
the buildup of plaque and raises the risk of heart attack, stroke, and heart
failure over time. Blood pressure is usually described as two numbers:
systolic (the pressure while the heart is beating) over diastolic (the
pressure while the heart is resting between beats).

A resting blood pressure that is persistently elevated -- generally at or
above 130/80 mm Hg on repeated readings -- is considered high, though
thresholds and treatment targets are set individually by a clinician based
on overall risk and other conditions. A single elevated reading, especially
one taken in a stressful setting like a clinic visit, does not by itself
indicate hypertension.

Lifestyle factors that influence blood pressure include sodium intake,
physical activity level, alcohol consumption, weight, and stress. Blood
pressure control is one of the most effective ways to reduce cardiovascular
risk, and is usually addressed through a combination of lifestyle changes and,
when needed, medication prescribed and monitored by a healthcare provider.
