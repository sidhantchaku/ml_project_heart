---
title: Blood Sugar, Diabetes, and Heart Disease
source_organization: CDC

Over time, elevated blood sugar damages blood vessels and the nerves that
control the heart, which is why diabetes roughly doubles the risk of heart
disease compared to people without diabetes. High fasting blood sugar --
commonly the threshold used is above 120 mg/dl in screening contexts, though
formal diabetes diagnosis criteria differ slightly -- is an early signal that
blood sugar regulation may already be impaired, even before a formal
diabetes diagnosis.

People with diabetes or elevated fasting blood sugar often also have other
risk factors clustered together, such as high blood pressure, abnormal
cholesterol, and excess weight around the abdomen -- sometimes referred to as
metabolic syndrome -- which compounds cardiovascular risk beyond what any
single factor would suggest alone.

Because the relationship between blood sugar and heart disease develops
gradually, routine screening (fasting blood sugar or HbA1c tests) is the
standard way this risk factor is identified, and management is typically a
combination of diet, physical activity, weight management, and, when
appropriate, medication supervised by a healthcare provider.
