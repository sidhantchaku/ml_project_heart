---
title: Physical Activity and Heart Health
source_organization: CDC
source_url: https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/benefits/heart-health.html
category: prevention
---

Regular physical activity strengthens the heart muscle, helps control blood
pressure and cholesterol, supports healthy blood sugar levels, and helps
maintain a healthy weight -- all of which lower cardiovascular risk. General
public health guidance recommends adults aim for at least 150 minutes per
week of moderate-intensity aerobic activity (such as brisk walking), or 75
minutes of vigorous-intensity activity, plus muscle-strengthening activity on
two or more days per week.

A useful indicator during activity is maximum heart rate achieved relative to
what would be expected for a person's age -- a notably low maximum heart rate
during exertion can sometimes reflect reduced cardiovascular fitness or an
underlying condition limiting the heart's response to exercise, though many
other factors (medications, fitness level, measurement conditions) also
affect this number.

People with existing heart conditions or significant risk factors should
discuss an appropriate activity plan with a healthcare provider before
starting or significantly increasing exercise intensity.
