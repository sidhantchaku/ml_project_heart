Coronary angiography is an imaging procedure that lets doctors see whether
and where the coronary arteries -- the vessels that supply blood directly to
the heart muscle -- have narrowed due to plaque buildup. The number of major
vessels showing significant narrowing is one way clinicians describe the
extent of coronary artery disease: involvement of more vessels generally
reflects more widespread disease and is associated with higher risk of
future cardiac events.

Vessel narrowing develops gradually as fatty deposits (atherosclerosis)
accumulate over years, often without any noticeable symptoms until the
narrowing becomes severe enough to limit blood flow, particularly during
exertion.

Imaging findings like this are typically only available after a clinical
procedure ordered by a physician based on symptoms, risk factors, or other
test results -- they are not something a patient can determine from
symptoms or a questionnaire alone, and they are always interpreted in the
context of the full clinical picture.
