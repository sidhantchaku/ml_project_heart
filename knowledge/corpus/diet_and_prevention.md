---
title: Diet and Cardiovascular Disease Prevention
source_organization: American Heart Association
source_url: https://www.heart.org/en/healthy-living/healthy-eating
category: prevention
---

Dietary patterns that emphasize vegetables, fruits, whole grains, lean
protein, and healthy fats -- while limiting sodium, added sugars, saturated
fat, and highly processed foods -- are associated with lower rates of heart
disease. These patterns support healthy blood pressure, cholesterol, and
blood sugar levels, which are the main biological pathways connecting diet
to cardiovascular risk.

No single food or nutrient determines heart disease risk on its own; overall
dietary pattern and consistency over years matter more than any individual
meal or short-term change. Moderating sodium intake in particular helps
manage blood pressure, and reducing saturated fat intake is commonly
recommended to help manage LDL cholesterol.

Dietary changes are usually most effective as part of a broader plan that
also includes physical activity, not smoking, and, when needed, medical
management -- a registered dietitian or physician can help tailor
recommendations to an individual's health status and other conditions.
