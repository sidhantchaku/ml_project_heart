---
title: Smoking and Cardiovascular Risk
source_organization: CDC
source_url: https://www.cdc.gov/tobacco/about/smoking-and-cardiovascular-disease.html
category: risk_factors
---

Smoking damages the lining of blood vessels, contributes to plaque buildup,
raises blood pressure, and reduces the amount of oxygen the blood can carry
-- all of which increase the risk of heart attack and stroke. Even
low-intensity or occasional smoking carries increased cardiovascular risk
compared to not smoking, and secondhand smoke exposure is also associated
with elevated risk for nonsmokers.

The good news, according to public health guidance, is that much of the
excess cardiovascular risk from smoking declines within a few years of
quitting, and continues to decrease over time compared with continued
smoking. Risk reduction after quitting is one of the most impactful,
well-documented changes a person can make for cardiovascular health.

Quitting support -- counseling, nicotine replacement therapy, and
medications -- is most effective when guided by a healthcare provider, since
approaches that work well vary from person to person.
