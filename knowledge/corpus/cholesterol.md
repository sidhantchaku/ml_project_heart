---
title: Cholesterol and Cardiovascular Risk
source_organization: NHLBI (National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute)
source_url: https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/high-blood-cholesterol
category: risk_factors
---

Cholesterol is a fat-like substance the body needs, but too much of certain
kinds circulating in the blood can build up in artery walls as plaque,
narrowing the arteries and restricting blood flow to the heart -- a process
called atherosclerosis. LDL ("bad") cholesterol is the type most strongly
linked to this buildup, while HDL ("good") cholesterol helps carry
cholesterol away from the arteries to the liver, where it's removed from the
body.

Total cholesterol readings above roughly 200 mg/dl are generally considered
borderline-to-high, though clinicians look at the full lipid panel (LDL, HDL,
triglycerides) together with age, blood pressure, smoking status, and other
factors rather than a single number in isolation.

Diet high in saturated fat, lack of physical activity, smoking, obesity, and
genetics (family history of high cholesterol) all influence cholesterol
levels. Because atherosclerosis develops silently over years, cholesterol is
usually checked through periodic blood tests rather than symptoms, and
management is individualized by a healthcare provider.
