---
title: Angina and Exercise-Induced Chest Pain
source_organization: American Heart Association

---

Angina is chest discomfort or pain that happens when the heart muscle isn't
getting enough oxygen-rich blood, often because one or more coronary arteries
are narrowed. It is a symptom, not a disease itself, and can feel like
pressure, squeezing, fullness, or pain in the chest, and sometimes in the
arms, neck, jaw, or back.

Exercise-induced angina refers to this discomfort appearing specifically
during physical exertion, when the heart's oxygen demand rises but narrowed
arteries limit how much oxygen-rich blood can reach the heart muscle. It
typically eases with rest. Because exertion increases the heart's workload in
a predictable way, exercise testing (sometimes called a stress test) is a
common way clinicians evaluate whether chest discomfort is related to reduced
blood flow to the heart.

Reporting exercise-induced chest discomfort to a healthcare provider is
important, since it can be an early indicator of narrowed coronary arteries
even when a person otherwise feels well at rest. It should not be self-
diagnosed or self-treated based on general information alone.
