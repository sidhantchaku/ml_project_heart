---
title: ST Depression and Exercise ECG Testing

During an exercise electrocardiogram (ECG) stress test, a measurement called
ST depression looks at how a specific part of the heart's electrical signal
(the ST segment) shifts as the heart works harder. A larger ST depression
during exercise, often reported as "oldpeak" in clinical data sets, can
suggest that part of the heart muscle isn't receiving enough blood flow
under exertion -- a pattern associated with narrowed coronary arteries.

This measurement is one piece of evidence among several a clinician
considers together with symptoms, resting ECG findings, imaging results, and
risk factors -- it is not a stand-alone diagnostic marker, and a normal or
mildly abnormal ST segment reading does not rule in or rule out coronary
artery disease by itself.

Stress testing is generally performed under medical supervision because
exercise itself places load on the cardiovascular system, and results are
always interpreted by a trained professional rather than by an automated
threshold in isolation.
